*geospell.txt*   A geophysics spelling dictionary you maintain in git   *geospell-plugin*

                                                *geospell* *plugin-geospell*
Vim's `en_us` spell checking, extended with the vocabulary of geophysics:
technical terms, acronyms, names from the literature. The dictionary is
generated from small tracked word lists, so the repository carries the
lists and the generator, never the compiled file. Lives in
plugin/geospell.vim and autoload/geospell.vim.

==============================================================================
1. Files                                                      *geospell-files*

spell/geophysics/words.txt      The vocabulary, one word per line. Tracked.
                                Sorted on every write; `#` starts a comment.
spell/geophysics/reject.txt     Words that must never be included, even if
                                a harvest finds them again. Tracked.
spell/geophysics/fetch-corpus.py
                                Downloads text to mine for candidates.
                                Tracked.
spell/geophysics/corpus/        The downloaded and extracted text. Not
                                tracked; regenerate with the script.
spell/geophysics.utf-8.spl      The compiled dictionary. Not tracked;
                                rebuilt automatically when the lists are
                                newer than it.

==============================================================================
2. Using the dictionary                                       *geospell-usage*

The vimrc sets: >
    set spelllang=en_us,geophysics
<
so `:set spell` checks against both. A word is accepted if either list knows
it. Case follows Vim's rules: a lowercase entry such as `magnetometer` also
accepts `Magnetometer` and `MAGNETOMETER`; an entry with capitals such as
`Bouguer` or `TMI` accepts only that spelling and its all-capitals form.

The usual keys keep working: `]s` and `[s` move between misspellings, `z=`
suggests, and `zg` adds a word to your personal `spell/en.utf-8.add`, which
is separate from this dictionary. Use `zg` for one-off words and
|:GeoSpellAdd| for vocabulary the field shares.

==============================================================================
3. Adding words                                                 *geospell-add*

One or a few words ~
                                                                *:GeoSpellAdd*
:GeoSpellAdd            Add the word under the cursor.
:GeoSpellAdd {words}    Add each word given.

The words go into words.txt, are removed from reject.txt if they were
there, and the dictionary is rebuilt at once, so the red underline goes away
without leaving Vim.

Many words at once ~
                                          *:GeoSpellHarvest* *:GeoSpellAccept*
:GeoSpellHarvest {paths}
                        Scan files for words `en_us` does not know and open
                        a review buffer listing them, most frequent first,
                        one `count<Tab>word` per line. {paths} can be files,
                        globs, or directories (searched recursively for
                        .tex .typ .md .txt .bib .rst .org). Words already in
                        words.txt or reject.txt are not listed, nor words
                        seen fewer than |g:geospell_min_count| times.
                        Without {paths} the corpus directory is scanned.

                        In the review buffer, delete the lines you do not
                        want. `dd` on a line, `:g/^\d\+\tSomeName/d` for a
                        pattern, or `:v/geo/d` to keep only lines matching
                        something. Then:

:GeoSpellAccept         Add every remaining word to words.txt, rebuild, and
                        close the review buffer.

A good habit after finishing a manuscript: `:GeoSpellHarvest %` in it and
accept what is really vocabulary.

By hand ~

Edit spell/geophysics/words.txt directly; it is plain text. The dictionary
rebuilds the next time Vim starts, or now with |:GeoSpellBuild|.

==============================================================================
4. Removing words                                            *geospell-remove*

                                                             *:GeoSpellRemove*
:GeoSpellRemove         Remove the word under the cursor.
:GeoSpellRemove {words} Remove each word given.

Removed words are appended to reject.txt so that no later harvest brings
them back; delete a line from reject.txt to allow the word again. Editing
either file by hand works as well; rebuild afterwards.

Why a reject list: the base list came partly from corpora, which contain
misspellings, OCR errors and other people's typos. When one of those turns
up as an accepted word, remove it once and it stays gone.

==============================================================================
5. Updating the base list                                    *geospell-update*

The base list was built from these sources:

- The user's own manuscripts and translations (Typst, LaTeX, BibTeX).
- The English side of Cherepovski's English-Russian dictionary of
  geophysics, extracted from the epub in Documents/kogan-translation.
- Wikipedia articles in the geophysics category tree and neighbouring
  categories: seismology, geodesy, geomagnetism, gravimetry, geophysical
  imaging, petroleum geology, mineral exploration, geostatistics, inverse
  problems, radiometric dating, remote sensing, structural geology,
  petrology, sedimentology, and for exploration and economic geology:
  economic geology, ore deposits, mining, mineralogy, geochemistry,
  hydrogeology and well logging. One dictionary covers all of it; Vim
  loads it per buffer at no noticeable cost, and the fields overlap too
  much to be worth separating.
- Titles and abstracts of recent arXiv physics.geo-ph submissions.

To refresh the corpus and re-harvest: >
    python spell/geophysics/fetch-corpus.py        " minutes; rate limited
    :GeoSpellHarvest                               " scans the corpus dir
<
then review and |:GeoSpellAccept|. Raise |g:geospell_min_count| for a
stricter harvest of a large corpus (3 or more is sensible for Wikipedia
text, 2 for your own writing). Words already accepted or rejected never
reappear, so a refresh only shows what is new.

To add a source of your own, put text files in spell/geophysics/corpus/
or pass their paths to |:GeoSpellHarvest|. The script's category list and
sizes are plain constants near the top of fetch-corpus.py.

==============================================================================
6. Commands and options                                    *geospell-commands*

:GeoSpellBuild                                                *:GeoSpellBuild*
                        Compile words.txt minus reject.txt into the .spl and
                        reload it in windows that use it.

:GeoSpellStats                                                *:GeoSpellStats*
                        Word and reject counts, and when the .spl was built.

                                                        *g:geospell_min_count*
g:geospell_min_count    Minimum occurrences for a harvested word to be
                        listed. Default 2.

                                                          *g:geospell_sources*
g:geospell_sources      List of paths scanned by |:GeoSpellHarvest| when no
                        paths are given. Default: the corpus directory.

==============================================================================
7. How the build works                                        *geospell-build*

|:mkspell| wants a Hunspell-style pair of files. The build writes a minimal
affix file containing only `SET UTF-8` and a .dic listing the words, into a
temporary directory, and runs
`:mkspell! spell/geophysics {tmp}/geophysics`. No affix rules are used:
inflected forms found in the sources are listed as separate words, which
keeps the list obvious and greppable. Building a few thousand words takes
well under a second and happens at startup whenever words.txt or reject.txt
is newer than the .spl.
